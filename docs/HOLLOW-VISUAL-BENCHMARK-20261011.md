# Hollow visual direction benchmark — 11 October 2026

**Better horror needs specific images that tell the story, consistent characters and deliberate reveals. More movement alone will not fix it.** Narration-first stills and animated scenes are both credible formats; they need different production plans.

## Evidence and cost

Re-ran `/watch` locally on six already downloaded public YouTube examples. Inspected **all 36 returned frames** and read the focused native automatic captions where available. Long sources were limited to relevant 110–120-second sections; two shorter videos were sampled over their complete runtime. No new downloads, models, paid calls or cloud audio uploads.

Popularity comes from cached primary YouTube metadata collected on 11 October: every source has more than 200K views in that snapshot. Counts may have changed. This is a purposeful comparison, not a global trending chart or proof of what caused the views. Each count and source-specific observation has one primary origin: that video and its platform metadata.

This is timestamped visual/caption analysis, **not continuous audiovisual listening**. It cannot establish voice acting, soundtrack quality, exact animation smoothness or every intervening action. ## Direction for original Hollow productions

These are editorial recommendations from the examples, not a measured recipe for virality:

- **Lock the story world first.** Define a character's appearance, clothes, recurring props, room layout, palette and illustration style once. Reuse those references across shots. Different characters should remain distinguishable without name labels.
- **Give every shot a job.** Establish the location; show the clue; reveal a reaction or choice; change the threat's proximity; show the consequence. A wide room view, medium character shot, over-shoulder angle and object insert provide variety because they communicate different information.
- **Write visible details.** Replace vague directions such as “creepy atmosphere” with a particular object, placement, lighting source and changed state. An intact door, a moved latch, then a hand where none should fit is more directable than three generic dark silhouettes.
- **Protect reveal order.** Avoid showing the full threat while narration is still describing uncertainty. Make normal state, anomaly and reveal visibly different. Let an empty doorway or still object hold anticipation when the story needs it.
- **Animate purposefully.** Use movement for a glance, door opening, object shift, approach or reveal. Endless zoom/shake on unrelated art is not character animation. A stable image can be the better choice when motion would damage identity or distract from narration.
- **Separate the two formats honestly.** Narrated illustrated stories can use strong stills and restrained camera changes. Animated stories need consistent assets and controlled actions. Do not present a moving slideshow as equivalent to complete scene animation.
- **Leave room for consequences.** A reveal is stronger when it changes a character's choice or later behavior. Keep that consequence comprehensible before ending.

An original scene plan can use: **what the viewer currently believes → concrete visible clue → character choice → new visible state → consequence**. Record shot type, subject, location, required prop, allowed motion and reveal state for each scene. This is a production checklist, not a requirement to make every story follow the same plot.

## What this does not prove

Good metadata or a better image model cannot establish that a finished story is enjoyable. The actual rendered video still needs checks for character continuity, scene/narration agreement, readable captions, coherent reveals and direct listening to the voice. This comparison makes no claim that YuE2's singing ability transfers to spoken acting or that a particular TTS model sounds human.

No source story, character design, voice or footage should be copied into Hollow. Transfer scene-direction principles and create original assets; generated fiction should not inherit other channels' TRUE labels.

## Gaps and verification

Six source-specific observations/counts are single-origin primary evidence, disclosed above. No private retention, revenue, broader trend velocity, controlled comparison or guarantee of 100K views was established. Automatic caption mistakes are possible. Four long-form/anthology examples were viewed only in the named focus ranges; their complete opening-to-ending arcs were not assessed. Two uncaptioned sources have visual evidence only. Metadata was reused from earlier the same day rather than refreshed.

The research ledger lint and its positive/negative-control self-test passed. No production code, app settings or publishing behavior was changed for this study.
