# Streamer clip study — 20 public sources

Observed October 11, 2026. Ten Twitch-associated and ten Kick-associated streamer Shorts were downloaded with the local watch workflow. Every selected frame was visually inspected and native YouTube automatic captions were read. This is **sparse frame-and-caption analysis, not uninterrupted audiovisual playback**; missed edits, tone and caption errors remain possible. Twitch evidence was inspected by the Twitch study agent; Kick evidence by the Kick study agent.

Selection was bounded to the latest 20 Shorts on four Twitch-associated channels and latest 15 on three selected Kick-associated channels; higher-view candidates were selected while retaining format variety and usable captions. Two captionless Twitch candidates were excluded. This is not a global trending list. Streamer association does not authenticate the original broadcast platform of every clip.

All numbers below are **public YouTube lifetime view snapshots**, not Twitch/Kick views, measured growth, retention or evidence that a title, hashtag or edit caused popularity. All snapshot dates are UTC on 2026-10-11; exact extraction times and timestamped evidence are retained in the local findings JSON.

## Apply the patterns
1. Start with an intelligible question, challenge, prediction or promise. Keep enough setup to make the reaction, reversal, victory or reveal understandable; louder is not automatically better.
2. Keep the payoff the actual cut earns. Preserve a gameplay prediction before the result, a disputed quote with its clarification, and a gift promise with its reveal. Trim empty desktop tails. Do not claim a win when sampled evidence shows continued play.
3. Frame the person and relevant action for vertical viewing. Stacked callers and face/game layouts repeatedly appeared; use them when both panels explain the moment. Color flashes, captions and zooms are observed techniques, not proven ranking factors.
4. Write original audience copy from this clip. Name the specific event, use natural topic terms, and attribute unverified claims. Avoid invented feud endings, pregnancy, professional hiring, cheating or real-family events inferred from hyperbole/gameplay.
5. Use new public snapshots and the channel’s own results to test format ideas. The study supports reusable hypotheses, not copying creator titles, a universal template or guaranteed growth. Empty descriptions and varied hashtag use in the sample prevent strong causal metadata conclusions.

## Evidence and implementation boundary
Local evidence: `artifacts/streamer-study/twitch-findings.json` and `kick-findings.json` contain full source metadata, timestamped observations, caption provenance, frame paths and limitations. Raw download artifacts are disposable and may contain media delivery URLs; the public source links above are the reusable references.
Current public-copy generation uses source speech from the selected cut and its own sampled video frames. Private judge notes, selection reasons, chat spikes and visual review prose are excluded from the writer brief. Style research is explicitly untrusted reference material rather than factual evidence about the candidate. If generation or validation fails, publication waits; internal captions are not a fallback.
The public-text guard blocks configured diagnostic phrases and substantial verbatim overlap with private notes, with an exemption for independently observed source dialogue. Fresh video-origin copy is required at posting boundaries. These defenses are practical checks, not complete paraphrase detection. See [YouTube publishing guidance](YOUTUBE-PUBLISHING.md).
