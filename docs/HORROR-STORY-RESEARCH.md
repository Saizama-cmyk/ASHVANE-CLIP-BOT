# Ashvane original horror studio research

Checked **11 October 2026**. This research informs a creation workflow separate from stream clipping: original fiction → director → writer → story jury → narration and visuals → rendered-video review → publishing queue → performance review.

## Evidence and limits

Inspected **118 selected frames from 14 successfully downloaded public videos**: **13 horror examples** and one creator’s production/monetization account. Ten videos supplied English automatic captions. Two long videos have focused opening samples plus three later cue frames each; the other samples span their full duration. Every selected frame was inspected in chronological contact sheets. This is sampled visual and caption analysis, **not continuous audiovisual playback**. Sound mixing, vocal expression, exact cut timing and audience retention were not measured.

The sample deliberately includes narrated stills, 2D character animation, analog collage, found-footage environments and creature micro-shorts. It is not a global trending list. Views below are exact saved extraction snapshots on 11 October, useful only as public reach context; they do not establish that a technique caused performance. Metadata, coverage, frame paths, source URLs and recommendations are in `artifacts/horror-study/findings.json`. Local video/caption evidence remains in the ignored artifacts directory; no creator footage, script or voice is a production asset.

## Creation system decisions

These are design recommendations, not experimentally proven retention rules.

- Keep **genre, visual treatment and story length independent**. Paranormal, psychological, creature, urban legend, folklore, cosmic, analog, sci-fi and survival can each use still-image narration or scene animation.
- The director plans an ordinary situation, concrete discrepancy, changing stakes, character decision, earned reveal and consequential last image. Not every story needs a monster or surprise twist.
- Each scene needs narration plus specific setting, subject and action. Maintain character appearance, room layout, time and recurring objects. An image chosen because it looks scary is insufficient if it contradicts the current scene.
- Shorts need their own setup and payoff. Do not publish the middle of an episode as though it were a complete story. Longer episodes can spend more time on motive, evidence and aftermath, but cannot fill time by repeating dread.
- Separate spoken manuscript, visual instructions, private jury notes and public copy in storage. Private ratings and reasoning must never enter narration, burned captions, title or description.
- Generate original fictional stories, not rewrites of researched scripts. Do not clone researched creators’ voices, reuse signature monsters or turn competitor titles into templates.
- The script jury checks intelligibility, continuity, agency, escalation, earned payoff, originality and duration plausibility. Passing a script is not proof that its rendered video is good.
- The final reviewer must inspect the actual render: intelligible narration, scene correspondence, reveal timing, caption accuracy/readability, audio presence, unexpected black gaps and final duration. Missing evidence blocks automatic publication rather than producing a fictional verdict.

The baseline renderer can honestly offer **original stylized shadow theatre** with changing character/action layers as animation, and **narrated illustrations** as nonanimated. A still with camera movement alone stays in the latter category. Offline system voices should be auditionable and labeled accurately; they do not imply a dramatic actor performance. Show a real output preview and surface provider readiness before promising higher-end generative video. Keep a stronger local narrator/image provider replaceable without changing the story/publish state machine.

## Public packaging and platform requirements

Each platform-policy statement below rests on a **single authoritative origin: YouTube/Google’s own documentation**, checked 11 October 2026. Several Google pages are not independent confirmation.

YouTube expects original, authentic work; repetitive or mass-produced material can be ineligible for monetization, and reused-content review remains distinct. An AI-assisted process does not by itself establish eligibility. Build substantive variation in characters, conflicts, scenes and outcomes; preserve the original project, source/license records and final render. [Channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)

Use an accurate premise-specific title and thumbnail. Give each story a unique description with its actual subject in the opening lines and one or two relevant concepts naturally expressed. Suggested original example: **“The Elevator Stopped at a Floor That Doesn’t Exist | Original Horror”**, followed by a brief spoiler-light premise and “An original fictional horror story.” Do not call generated fiction TRUE, confirmed, leaked footage or a real testimony. Tags mainly help with misspellings; prioritize title, thumbnail and description. Use a few genuinely relevant hashtags as an editorial choice, not a promised ranking trick. [Description guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449?hl=en), [Tags guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/146402?hl=en)

Realistic meaningfully generated/altered scenes require disclosure. Nonrealistic animation and script assistance generally do not; realistic fake events or impersonation need particular care. YouTube says disclosure itself does not remove monetization eligibility. Record the visual treatment and disclosure decision per project; the API exposes `status.containsSyntheticMedia`. [GenAI disclosure](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491), [Video resource](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos)

For standard channels, current square/vertical uploads up to three minutes can be Shorts. A Short over one minute with an active copyright claim is globally blocked. Use original or properly licensed sound/music and do not assume a track available inside a consumer editing app is licensed for offline automated distribution. Default production lengths can be narrower for practicality; those are product choices, not YouTube’s maximum. [Three-minute Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)

Horror is not exempt from ad-suitability rules. Graphic injury, gore and highly graphic spoken descriptions can restrict advertising. Atmospheric threat and implied consequences are a sensible initial production direction, not a guarantee of ads or income. Animation alone does not mean child-directed content. [Advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)

## Separate automatic publishing workflow

Creation needs its own durable project and delivery records, not fake clip candidates. Recommended states: drafting → reviewing → rendering → final review → ready → queued → uploading → published, with visible failed/held/interrupted states. Bind the selected destination account when queuing. Do not silently use a different account after an authentication change.

The user has requested automatic posting. Make that a real delivery worker: require a finished file and final review, snapshot final public metadata/disclosure/audience/privacy settings, hash the file, persist an attempt before network calls, upload, then persist the remote video ID/URL. Once the platform accepts an upload, do not repeat `videos.insert` merely because a later response was lost. Treat uncertain acceptance as reconciliation/attention, not a blind retry. These are engineering recommendations, not guarantees that a platform provides an idempotency key.

Google supports authorized video upload and resumable transfer. A resumed session is useful after network interruption; distinguish transfer errors from revoked authorization and policy rejection. Scheduled `publishAt` requires private, never-before-published content; a past timestamp publishes immediately. Set privacy explicitly. Show missing connections, quota/rate errors and retry times in the interface. [Upload API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos/insert), [Resumable protocol](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/guides/using_resumable_upload_protocol), [Video scheduling fields](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos)

After publication, record actual destination/metadata/video ID and evaluate the channel’s own retention, viewed-vs-swiped behavior where available, comments and returns by genre/length/treatment. Public competitor views cannot reveal those internal metrics. Keep research observations separate from automatically changing production settings; require enough comparable own-channel evidence and reversible changes. Avoid automatically increasing publishing volume merely because a single video gets views.
